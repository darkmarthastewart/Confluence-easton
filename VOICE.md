# How Confluence Easton sounds

Two voices, one app.

**The neighbor** handles everyday moments. Think of a friend who's lived downtown for twenty years: warm, a little funny, quick to point you somewhere good.

**The river** handles big moments. It's calm and unhurried, and it uses the imagery of two rivers meeting and things flowing together.

## When to use which

| Moment | Voice | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greetings, headings, empty screens, help text | Neighbor | "Evening, Easton. Here's what's on around the Circle." |
| Sign-up page opener, thanks page, guide openers, featured artist, the riverfront | River | "Two rivers meet here and become one. So do we." |
| Buttons and labels | Neither. Plain and literal | "Add to my plan", "Directions", "Send my listing" |

Most screens are neighbor with one river line at most. If a screen has two river lines, cut one.

## The neighbor

- Say "you" and "we". Use contractions.
- Use local names, but make sure visitors can follow them: "the Circle (Centre Square)" the first time on a page, then "the Circle".
- Be specific: "Come early for the best pick," not "Don't miss out!"
- One small joke per screen, at most. Never at anyone's expense.
- Empty screens should send people somewhere: "Quiet week on the calendar. Wander anyway."

## The river

- Short, calm sentences. No exclamation points.
- Water imagery stays light: meeting, flowing, current, the water, the Forks. One image per line.
- Keep it true. The Lehigh does meet the Delaware at Scott Park; nothing else needs dressing up.

## Dark Martha Stewart's brand

Her brand shows in how the app looks, not in extra words: the ballpoint ink trail, her lime, the research assistant drawn in chalk, the hexagon maker's mark. No margin-note jokes, asides or "fun facts" in the copy. Every line on a screen does a job.

## Never

- Hype or sales talk: "amazing", "don't miss", "hidden gem", "vibrant", "unique".
- Corporate words: "solutions", "leverage", "stakeholders", "platform".
- Claims we can't stand behind: "the best pizza in Easton". Say what's true instead: "Wood-fired, with high chairs."
- Exclamation points in river lines, and more than one per screen anywhere.

## Writing a listing

Listings are written in the business's own words, tidied up into the neighbor voice: one or two sentences, what it is and why you'd go. Keep their facts; don't invent praise.

- Before: "Premier destination for artisanal coffee experiences."
- After: "Single-origin pour-overs and big tables for laptops."
